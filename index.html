Spiral Spines

Stable in the Face of Disruption — Climate Biennale Vienna 2026

Regenerative MaterialVernacular Craft1:1 Prototype

A regenerative material practice that transforms uprooted and discarded timber — ecological disruption made structural. Traditional Afghan woodcarving techniques are adapted as a contemporary method for material processing, embedding collective narratives into reclaimed structural columns through a universal semiotic language inspired by ancient scripts.

Woven Realities

The Diversity of Afghan Homes — Climate Biennale Vienna 2026

Circular ArchitectureModular AssemblyAfghan Heritage

A 1:1-scale architectural prototype translating the intimate dimensions of Afghan dwelling into circular material practice. The structure substitutes traditional earth with reusable by-products from the construction and consumer waste sectors — Heraklith wood wool panels, chipboard, stones, and textiles — assembled through low-tech modular means.

The New Cairn

Heterogeneous Rubble Stacking for Post-Disaster Contexts

Rubble ReuseCompression-OnlyPost-Conflict

A research and construction project proposing whole, un-crushed demolition rubble — reinforced concrete, non-reinforced concrete, stone cladding — as masonry units in compression-only enclosures. The project develops a destruction-mode typology, a field-legible sorting system, and a practitioner handbook deployable in post-disaster Palestine and Afghanistan without specialist equipment.

Carbonfloss

eVolo Skyscraper Competition — Honorable Mention

SpeculativeEcologicalPolitical Statement

Carbonfloss is a political statement against absurd reluctance policies, spurring hope for action as reassurance for humanity amid impending environmental disaster. There's no room for hope when there is no action. Alarms of demise are ignored. When hope is scarce due to inaction towards an immediate and radical response, comes carbon floss as a political statement.

Medius paedagogicus

Published in Dazeen

Pedagogical SpaceInstitutionalFlat Hierarchy

A prototype tracing its character from Diola compounds in Casamance, Senegal — a covered radial arrangement of volumes connected by circular paths around an open shared area. The alternating volumes strategy allows for uninterrupted interconnected passage through classes and facilities alike, as a physical manifestation of equality and flat hierarchy.
