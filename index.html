Spiral Spines

Stable in the Face of Disruption — Climate Biennale Vienna 2026

Regenerative Material Vernacular Craft 1:1 Prototype Reclaimed Timber

Woven Realities

The Diversity of Afghan Homes

Circular Architecture Modular Assembly Socio-Spatial Afghan Heritage

A 1:1-scale architectural prototype translating the intimate dimensions of Afghan dwelling into circular material practice. Drawing on the spatial memory of clay and mud-brick vernacular, the structure substitutes traditional earth with reusable by-products from the construction and consumer waste sectors — Heraklith wood wool panels, chipboard, stones, and textiles — assembled through low-tech modular means.

Dimensions 666.5 × 322.5 × 263 cm
Capacity Up to 10 people
Materials Wood, Cement, Stone, Fabric
Technique Low-Tech Modular, Kinetic Base

The New Cairn

Heterogeneous Rubble Stacking for Post-Disaster Contexts

Rubble Reuse Compression-Only Post-Conflict Low-Tech Protocol

A research and construction project proposing whole, un-crushed demolition rubble — reinforced concrete, non-reinforced concrete, stone cladding — as masonry units in compression-only enclosures. The project develops a destruction-mode typology, a field-legible sorting system, and a practitioner handbook deployable in post-disaster Palestine and Afghanistan without specialist equipment.

Contexts Palestine · Afghanistan
Phase Research / Phase 1
Structural Logic Compression-Only / Safe Theorem
Collaborators Riwaq · SAWE · EPFL

About

Architecture as a medium for structural resilience and collective memory.

My practice operates at the boundary between research and construction — where material science meets vernacular tradition, and where the political conditions of destruction become the starting point for design.

I am interested in how post-disaster contexts generate their own material logic: the specific geometry of conflict rubble, the knowledge embedded in Afghan woodcarving, the structural potential of the circular waste economy. My work tests these hypotheses at 1:1 scale, in real conditions, with real communities.

Current research is focused on developing a generalisable, low-tech construction system for compression-only rubble enclosures — deployable in the Palestinian territories and post-earthquake Afghanistan without specialist equipment or industrial material inputs.

Based Nablus, West Bank · Vienna
Focus Regenerative material practice, post-conflict reconstruction, vernacular knowledge systems
Collaborators Riwaq (Ramallah) · SAWE / Engr. Sahar Mahmood · Dr. Tayebeh Nazarian · EPFL SXL
Exhibitions Climate Biennale Vienna 2026 — Brunnenpassage
Approach Technology-informed, low-tech deployment. Research phase uses 3D scanning and structural analysis; deployment requires only hand tools and visual sorting.
